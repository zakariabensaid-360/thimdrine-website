About us and how we started

The Mountain Harvest & The Sacred Oils

The Story of Yamina’s High-Altitude Harvest Deep in the craggy peaks of the Rif Mountains, long before the morning fog clears from the valleys, Yamina begins her trek. Wearing her traditional chelham and woven headscarf, she walks along ancient mountain paths carved into the terraced slopes. The Rif region is renowned for its rich biodiversity—a natural sanctuary filled with wild lavender (Lavandula stoechas), mountain thyme (Za'atar), rosemary, and wild sage. For generations, women like Yamina have held the secret knowledge of the mountains. Harvesting is done entirely by hand using small copper sickles. She never over-harvests a single patch: half the flowers are left for the mountain bees, and only the mature blooms are collected in woven esparto baskets slung across her donkey’s back.

The Art of Earth Soap & Herbal Poultices

The Story of Malika in the Stone Courtyard In a quiet courtyard surrounded by terracotta walls, whitewashed homes, and grazing mountain goats, Malika sits at her heavy wooden worktable. Her hands bear the light stains of crushed herbs and natural clays. In the Rif, soap-making and remedy prep are seasonal rituals where community and craftsmanship meet. Children and farm animals wander through the sunlit terrace while Malika prepares formulations that have protected mountain families from harsh winter winds for centuries. Malika relies on Ghassoul (natural Moroccan clay enriched with minerals) and saponin-rich wild plants like soapwort (Tighaghatin) that grow abundantly near mountain streams.

The Sanctuary of the Herbalist’s Kitchen

The Story of Fatima’s Mountain Workshop When winter clouds gather over the mountain ridges, Fatima moves her workshop indoors. Inside her mud-brick house, bundles of dried eucalyptus, chamomile, and wild marjoram hang from the exposed wooden ceiling beams. Large ceramic jugs, polished copper bowls, and stone mortars line the rustic shelves. Here, Fatima transforms raw mountain ingredients into soothing salves, floral facial toners, and traditional remedies for trade with surrounding villages and visitors seeking authentic Amazigh wellness preparations.
